fMRI Quality Assurance (QA) tool theory

Get an introduction to the fMRI Quality Assurance (QA) tool. Learn about the variables gathered in the QA procedure, and the six plots the tool creates.

Introduction

The fMRI QA tool assesses the stability of the MRI system. The tool measures, analyzes, plots, and reports data about long-term image temporal stability of the scanner in conformance with the fBIRN consortium. It also shows background noise, peak-to-peak stability, signal drift, mean ghost, mean ghost drift (in addition to the drift), SNR, SFNR, and mean including Weiskoff stability.

The tool runs the procedure on a spherical phantom (TLT head, MRS, or agar) for an extended period and calculates statistics from the resulting images. These statistics give an assessment of the system’s stability. When the stability is poor, the statistics can be used to locate the root cause. The tool can also be run routinely to monitor system stability. There are no pass/fail criteria or specifications for this test. Work with the site to identify if the results are within their tolerances.

fMRI QA tool

The fMRI QA tool takes an fMRI series of phantom images and outputs 6 plots and values of 10 variables, which are then used to assess the system stability and ghosting levels.

The fMRI QA tool can be run in two modes: Basic and Expert. Basic mode does automatic acquisition using fBIRN protocol, analyzes the images, reports results, and archives the results without user intervention. Expert mode gives more user interaction, where the user can load any protocol, load and view images, do stability and ghosting analysis, archive data, and view protocol details. Both modes are outlined below.

Variables gathered in fMRI QA procedure

The table shows the types of fMRI QA variable values that are particularly useful in tracking scanner performance and identifying scanner malfunction.

fMRI QA tool variables
Report measurementVariableDefinition
RMSRMS stabilityTemporal standard deviation of the detrended mean ROI signal normalized by the average signal and converted to percentage. RMS stability is equivalent to the last point in the Weisskoff curve.
PeakToPeakPeak-to-peak fluctuationMeasures the worst case temporal fluctuation in percentage.
SFNRSignal-to-fluctuation-noise ratioSFNR, also called temporal signal-to-noise ratio (TSNR), measures average single-pixel fluctuation.
RDCRadius of decorrelation (RDC)RDC, also called decorrelation distance, is a quantitative measure of how spatially correlated is the fluctuation noise in the fMRI images.
DriftSignal driftSignal drift, or simply drift, measures change of average signal intensity over the fMRI QA scan. It is defined as the absolute value of difference between the last and first points of the fitted mean ROI curve normalized by the mean signal and then converted to percentage.
SNRSignal-to-noise ratio (SNR)The spatial SNR is also measured as part of the fMRI QA report.
MeanGhostMean ghostTemporal average of the mean ghost versus time curve.
MeanGhostDriftMean ghost driftAbsolute difference of the last and first point mean ghosting level.
TopGhostTop ghostTemporal average of the top 10% ghost versus time curve.
TopGhostDriftTop ghost driftAbsolute difference of the last and first point top ghosting level.

Plots created by fMRI QA tool

This section lists the definition of the six plots created by the fMRI QA tool.

fMRI QA tool plots
VariableDefinition
Mean ROI vs. TimeTwo curves are plotted in the mean ROI versus time plot, measured and fitted.
Detrended Mean vs. TimeThe detrended mean versus time plot shows the noise fluctuation in percentage after the overall signal trended is removed.
Fluctuation Freq SpectrumThis plot shows fluctuation frequency spectrum, and is helpful in capturing the frequency of the major oscillating components in fMRI images.
Weisskoff CurveWeisskoff curve measures system stability at different ROI sizes, therefore implying the spatial correlation of the instability or noise. Perfectly spatially uncorrelated Gaussian noise is a straight green line. The measured (blue line) can deviate from the ideal line. The deviation reflects how much spatial correlation there is in the fluctuation or noise.
Mean Ghost vs. TimeThe mean ghost versus time curve measures the average Nyquist ghosting level within the ghosting ROI (blue line) over time. In the fMRI QA tool, mean ghost normalized by the average signal is shown in green.
Top 10% Ghost vs. TimeThe top 10% ghost versus time curve measures spatial peak Nyquist ghosting level over time.
