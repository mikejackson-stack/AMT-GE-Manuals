Deionizing the power electronics coolant

Follow these steps to deionize and filter the power electronics coolant in the Integrated Cooling Cabinet (ICC).

About this task

Like the gradient coil coolant, the power electronics coolant should also be filtered using the deionization kit in order to keep the coolant clean and to refresh the coolant additives.

Procedure

  1. Remove the cap from the Cabinet Cooling Unit (CCU) tank fill port. Connect the tank funnel extension to the fill port and thread the deionization tank adapter into it. Open the vent on the tank adapter.
  2. If it is not already off, remove the yellow cap from the CCU drain that is labeled drain valve dirt trap. Then connect the deionization kit suction line to the dirt separator drain port.
  3. Connect the deionization kit return line quick disconnect to the deionization tank adapter.
  4. Turn on the service outlet breaker.
  5. Monitor the suction line.
    Coolant should be trickling into the suction line.
    • If coolant is in the suction line at the pump, the pump is sufficiently primed.
    • If no coolant is in the suction line, make sure that the return line is connected properly to the CCU tank fill port. See note below.
    DO NOT attempt to run the deionization kit pump until the pump is primed.
    Note: Briefly start the pump for 3 to 5 seconds until it has caught prime. The pump must be primed to prevent damage. Make sure there is flow by observing the coolant filling the deionization and filter housings. If flow stops, stop the pump to prevent damage. Disconnect the line from the reservoir. Remove the filter housing and drain.
    • Make sure the filter tool is on the floor below ICC tanks
    • Make sure hoses are not kinked
    • Make sure the vent on the return hose is open
    • Repeat this step until the pump is primed
  6. Run the deionization kit for 30 minutes and then turn off the service outlet breaker.
    Notice: If you leave the system for 1 hour without doing anything, the system will be in standby mode and the CCU will be turned off. To avoid this, enter a new exam, enter the appropriate information, and then select Start Exam.
  7. Disconnect the deionization kit hoses.
    Note: Coolant may spill out from the hoses when disconnecting. Put a service tank within reach before disconnecting.
    1. Disconnect the deionization kit return line from the tank adapter on the CCU tank fill port. Then drain the hose into the service tank.
    2. Disconnect the deionization kit suction line from the CCU dirt separator drain port. Then connect the hose to the DVMR pump.
    3. Pump out the remaining deionization kit coolant into the service tank.
    4. Remove the DVMR pump from the deionization kit suction line.
  8. Remove the deionization tank adapter from the CCU tank fill port.
    CAUTION
    Hazardous chemicals
    Chemicals may cause injury.
    Wear goggles and gloves when pouring chemicals.
  9. Put the funnel into the CCU tank fill port and restore coolant from the service tank to the CCU tank.
  10. Locate the power electronics deionization chemical additive and pour one-half of the bottle into the CCU tank.
    Note: Do NOT use the whole bottle. Only use one-half.
    Note: Dispose of the deionization chemicals per local regulation.
  11. Remove the funnel and replace the cap on the CCU tank fill port.
  12. Drain the deionization kit return line and replace the filter as described in Replacing the deionization kit filter.
