About this task

The High Speed Stability (HSS) test evaluates the effects of vibration, or other rapid environmental or system phenomena, on the stability of the MRI process or on the hardware used to receive and process MRI signals.

The Grafidy kit (preferred) or the Universal SST kit can be used to do the HSS scan. Using the grafidy fixture to collect the HSS signals simplifies setup and test execution.

The HSS Pulse Sequence Database (PSD) drives the SIGNA Exciter with a rectangular RF pulse that is modulated to full scale to create a simple Free Induction Decay (FID) and collects the resultant FID with the SIGNA receiver. The FID is used to measure the frequency deviation or phase deviation over time. A real Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) is done on the deviation data to produce a frequency spectrum of any magnetic field disturbance within the selected bandwidth for the test.

The HSS PSD communicates with the LX IPG or Excite MGD to take control of the RF and gradient amplifiers from SIGNA. The gradient amplifiers can contribute noise at 30, 60, 120, and 180 Hz frequencies. The gradient amplifiers are put in standby (through the HSS PSD) to eliminate potential gradient electrical noise from interfering with the vibration-related sources of noise, which we are primarily interested in identifying. To identify the rotational frequency (RPM) of equipment such as fans and blowers, multiply the frequency by 60; for example, a peak located at 18 Hz may be caused by something rotating at 1080 RPM.

The HSS tool acts as a high-speed magnetometer, providing B0 field measurements. As the tool puts the gradient amplifiers in standby to remove any noise, the only things that affect B0 are vibration, magnet pressure, and moving metal items (EMI). The tool applies to all field strengths, magnets, and system configurations.

The tool uses the most basic pulse sequence (alpha flip) to create a simple FID. The tool also use a narrow RF Tx pulse-width to accommodate large B0 field inhomogeneity. Use the HSS tool to create a Frequency Deviation versus Time plot, a frequency deviation FFT spectrum (mag versus frequency of disturbance). The tool does not plot the position of the sample in the bore. The slope of the phase that represents the frequency of the sample is shown below.

Because the HSS tool takes the gradients off-line, which results in the gradients not pulsing during the use of the tool, the tool does not measure or diagnose gradient-induced vibration. The tool cannot be used to diagnose any of the following:

  • Gradient coil movements
  • RF coil movements
  • Patient cradle motions
  • Magnet motions that are driven by the gradients
  • Gradient subsystem instabilities
