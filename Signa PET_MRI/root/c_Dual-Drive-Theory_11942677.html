Dual drive theory

Topic ID: id_SL3977044-1131169

Overview

The purpose of the dual drive feature is to improve image homogeneity.

Dual drive technology reduces shading caused by dielectric affect when scanning areas of the body that are more elliptical or irregular in shape (rather than circular), such as the thigh, pelvis, abdomen, and breast. The dual drive controls the amplitude and phase relationships between the two separate drive points on the system PET insert and minimizes the dielectric effect. The dual drive system establishes an elliptical polarized RF field primarily in a horizontal direction.

Dual drive applies only to body transmit. Two identical body transmit paths are maintained from the exciter output to the PET insert input. Dual drive only affects body mode imaging and receive-only surface coil (single channel and phased array) imaging.

A PETMR system with dual drive amplifier and exciter drives the PET insert in either Quadrature mode or Elliptical mode.

Elliptical mode is available as a customer-purchased option. Quadrature mode is always available.

Two types of Elliptical mode are selectable in the interface:

  • Preset mode: A fixed set of values for attenuation and phase; the default mode for the end user in most scans. The presets vary based on the coil being used for a scan.
  • Optimized mode: The operator sets the values of attenuation and phase on a per-patient and per-anatomy basis. Using Optimized mode is complicated and time consuming. Most operators do not use this option.

Most system tools and service protocols are run only in Quadrature mode. One exception is Uniformity, which lets the user choose Quadrature or Preset mode.

Anatomies with circular shapes, such as head, knees, and wrists, are not improved through the use of dual drive technology. Therefore, dual drive is not applicable to any local TR coils (TR split head, TR knee, TR wrist, and so on).

The dual drive feature includes system tools for testing and calibration.

Topic ID: id_SL3977048-1131169

Hardware components

There are differences in some of the hardware components in the transmit chain for dual drive systems and single drive systems. However, in the receive chain, only the body receive quadrature preamplifier/combiner module is different.

3.0T systems with dual drive do not have a remotely located dummy load (in the hybrid or magnet room) to absorb reflected power. Instead, all reflected power from the RF PET insert is absorbed by use of internal circulators and dummy loads inside the RF amplifier. Therefore, during normal operation, it is not unusual to have large values of reflected power in excess of 4:1 VSWR on the channel 1 and channel 2 body RF transmission lines. The 3.0T DTX1 exciter drives the channel 1 and channel 2 RF amplifier gain stages with two RF outputs, each of which can be independently set with respect to amplitude and phase. However, both outputs use the same exciter unblank control signal.

In dual drive, the naming convention of CH1 for channel 1 and CH2 for channel 2 is used. CH2 exits the exciter at J13 and leads with respect to CH1 (at J12). CH2 is the adjustable channel for elliptical mode. The dual drive RF amplifier has two independent RF inputs and uses one unblank signal to control dual body output ports. Each body output port is capable of 15 kW peak envelope power (PEP) at 1 kW average.

During body transmit, the RF amplifier receives two inputs from the exciter (J14 and J21), and there are two outputs on J4 and J22. During head transmit, the RF amplifier receives one input from the exciter (J14), and there is one output on J3.

The driver module provides a single body TR bias signal to the dual drive RF amplifier, as in single drive systems. The amplifier couples this single bias signal onto the two body transmit output ports. Therefore, both body channel transmit cables carry both RF and common body TR DC signal (as in previous systems). The difference is an open or short-circuit failure on either CH1 or CH2 transmission line can cause a body TR bias fault. Locating the source of a fault involves using the TR DD System Path Check diagnostic and comparing the reported body TR bias current values while disconnecting the CH1 and CH2 transmission lines one at a time.

Topic ID: id_SL3986008-1131169

Dual drive RF amplifier

The dual drive RF amplifier transmits on two separate body channels (J4 and J22) or a head channel (J3). Each channel must be calibrated separately. Both body channels are calibrated for 15 kW at 127.72 MHz.

One is used for body channel 1 and head; the other is used for body channel 2. The amplifier has two digital rotary encoder adjustment controls, located on the front of the RF amplifier. One is used to adjust power output for body channel 1 and head; the other is used to adjust power output for body channel 2. Because these are digital devices, adjust them only while the RF amplifier is actively pulsing.

Topic ID: id_SL3986009-1131169

Driver module

Because both CH1 and CH2 are identical transmission line circuits and the body TR bias is being supplied in parallel fashion between both circuits, you should expect to see the same amount of bias drawn on each circuit when the other circuit is physically disconnected. When an open circuit fault is reported, then the fault is located on the channel circuit reporting the least amount of current during the TR DD System Path Check diagnostic when the other channel is disconnected. Likewise, when a short circuit is reported, then the fault is located on the channel circuit reporting the most amount of current during the TR DD System Path Check diagnostic when the other channel is disconnected.

Topic ID: id_SL11964180-1131169

Dynamic disable biasing

  • Driver module BD1, BD2, BD3, and BD4 dynamic disable bias are routed directly to the PET insert.
  • Reverse bias voltage (550 VDC), which syncs current through the PET insert tuning circuits and tunes the PET insert, is applied during body mode.
  • Forward bias voltage (-15 VDC) detunes the PET insert and enables head mode.
  • Reverse bias voltage (550 VDC), which enables the transmit and receive connection path to the PET insert, is applied during body mode.
  • Forward bias voltage (-15 VDC) isolates and decouples PET insert ports during head or surface mode.
Topic ID: id_SL3986010-1131169

Dual drive transmit receive switch (DTRSW)

The dual drive transmit receive switch (DTRSW) contains the body TR circuitry needed to properly route the transmit and receive signals and to protect the body preamplifiers and combiner assembly from signal overload. It does not contain quadrature phase shifting circuitry because this function is performed by the 3.0T DTX1 exciter.

The DTRSW uses direct drive bias to isolate PET insert ports during head and surface modes. Dynamic disable bias is not routed to the DTRSW and is instead routed directly to four existing dynamic disable lines at the PET insert.

Topic ID: id_SL12141922-1131169

DTRSW operation

For the dual drive feature, the DTRSW replaces the body hybrid. The DTRSW is mounted on the right side of the magnet near the back (opposite from where the body hybrid is on single drive systems). The preamplifier combiner assembly is mounted on it.

The head transmit circuit routing is identical to that used in 3.0T systems with the 35 kW RF amplifier, and the same components are used. The head transmit circuit is a single drive and does not use dual drive or a DTRSW.

Topic ID: id_SL3986012-1131169

Narrow band exciter

The 3.0T DTX exciter is the same basic exciter used on 3.0T systems with the 35 kW RF amplifier, but with two independent RF output channels. It also has a different part number.

Both channels are calibrated with each TPS reset. Each channel’s output is calibrated for amplitude and phase using the system receive loopback functionality. Three log files are generated: dtx1_cal_I_date-stamp, dtx1_cal_Q_date-stamp, and dtx1_iqPhase_date-stamp, all in /usr/g/service/log.

Topic ID: id_SL3986013-1131169

Quadrature preamplifier/combiner module

The 3.0T quadrature preamplifier combiner is the first gain/processing block in the PET insert receive chain (RxC). The preamplifier combiner accepts two inputs: the I and Q channels from the PET insert. The preamplifier combiner has a single output that connects to the RF hub. The function of the preamplifier combiner is similar to the preamplifier combiner used in single drive systems.

The high level function of the preamplifier combiner can be divided into two sections: the preamplifier and the IQ combiner. The preamplifier provides a nominal gain of 28 dB with a low level of injected noise. The IQ combiner combines the two amplified PET insert channels (I and Q) in quadrature to produce one composite output.

Software

Topic ID: id_SL3977050-1131169

Dual drive configuration files

MRConfig.cfg in /w/config has the amplifier type for dual drive as well as three additional parameters to enable dual drive: RFampType, dualDriveCapable, and ellipticDriveEnable.

Topic ID: id_SL3977051-1131169

Scan Rx

A parameter called RF Drive Mode is available on the scan prescription page. Quadrature or Preset can be selected on almost any scan sequence without any preliminary work. Preset is usually the default value.

Topic ID: id_SL3983618-1131169

Tools affected by dual drive

The following service tools are affected by dual drive:

  • RF/Universal Power Monitor (UPM) calibration
  • RF amplifier power check tool
  • RF amplifier (RFA)
  • Uniformity tool
  • Dual drive quadrature calibration

Dual Drive RF Amplifier Troubleshooting contains power check procedures to troubleshoot the dual drive RF amplifier.

Topic ID: id_SL3983619-1131169

RF amplifier and UPM calibration

The UPM tool user interface includes the setup for RF amplifier calibration.

Apbcal PSD ignores any values in the QuadratureTransmit.dat file, so no attenuation or phase is added during calibration.

With dual drive, the system has two body channels and one head channel. The additional body channel, along with the other two channels, needs calibrating and requires a functional check.

Topic ID: id_SL3983620-1131169

RF amplifier power check tool

The RF amplifier power check tool is used to understand the health of the RF amplifier and the rest of the transmit chain. It includes data for the additional body channel. Test the RF amplifier independently from the transmit chain hardware by connecting the body transmit channel (the one that you are testing) directly to an air-cooled RF dummy load. For more information, see RF Amplifier power check.

Topic ID: id_SL3983621-1131169

RFA

RFA includes the additional channel at exciter and amplifier output. However, it captures data on one channel at a time. It does allow the “unused” channel to be enabled or disabled. For more information, see Doing the Radio Frequency Analysis (RFA) Loopback test.

Topic ID: id_SL3983622-1131169

Uniformity tool

The user interface for the Uniformity Tool allows testing in Quad or Preset mode with PASS/FAIL results based on different limits: Quad > 15 and Preset > 30.

Topic ID: id_SL3983623-1131169

Dual drive quadrature calibration tool

The dual drive quadrature calibration tool is designed to determine the attenuation and phase values needed to create a quadrature field inside the bore. It compensates for non-idealities in the exciter, cabling, amplifier, DTRSW, and PET insert. For quadrature operation, the goal is to have two equal amplitude body transmit paths that are nominally 90 degrees apart. See Dual drive quadrature tool.
