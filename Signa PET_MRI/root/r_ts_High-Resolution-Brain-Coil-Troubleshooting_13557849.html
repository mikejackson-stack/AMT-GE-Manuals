PET/MR 8-channel high resolution brain coil troubleshooting

Topic ID: id_SL13401669-1243983

Personnel Requirements

Required Persons Procedure
130 minutes
Topic ID: id_SL13401670-1243983

Overview

Use the following suggestions to troubleshoot common problems with the 3.0T PET/MR 8-Channel High Resolution Brain Array Coil by Invivo (M8000RA).

Topic ID: id_SL13401671-1243983

Tools and Test Equipment

Digital Volt Meter (DVM)

Topic ID: id_SL13401672-1243983

Procedure

Troubleshooting should only be performed by a General Electric authorized Service Engineer. Make sure the cable for the Brain Coil is securely connected before proceeding.

Topic ID: id_SL13760510-1243983

Mechanical Hardware Check

  1. Lift the coil latches on both sides of the coil, and slide the coil to its superior and inferior extents. The coil should slide freely on the base with no binding or grinding.

  2. Make sure that the coil does not move on the baseplate when the latches are locked down.

Topic ID: id_SL13401860-1243983

Receiving No Signal

Problem: Unable to pre-scan or scanning, but not receiving a signal.

Possible Solutions:

  1. Verify the green light above Port A is illuminated, which indicates the coil is properly plugged into the system.

  2. Verify the system correctly detected the ‘PETMR 8Ch High Res Brain Array by Invivo’ by checking that it is selected in the Coils column on the Coils Selection screen shown below.

  3. Verify the landmark is correct and the cradle did not unlatch.

  4. Verify the scan locations and any Field of View (FOV) offsets are correct.

  5. Perform a cable continuity check as described in RF Signal Channels Check. This check must be performed by a General Electric (GE) authorized Service Engineer.

  6. Verify the coil is positioned with the cable exiting towards the bore and the system and coil polarity match.

  7. If still unable to receive a signal, remove the Brain Coil from the magnet bore, and attempt to scan (transmit and receive) using a different coil.

    • If the scan completes successfully, a problem may exist with the Brain Coil.

    • If unable to scan with the substitute coil, there may be a system problem related to this particular coil type.

    • If still not receiving a signal, the problem may be with the PET/MR System.

    Contact GE for further assistance.

Topic ID: id_SL13401872-1243983

Image Quality

Problem: Poor image quality, shaded images, or the multi-coil quality assurance test fails.

Possible Solutions:

  1. Perform a cable continuity check as described in RF Signal Channels Check. This check must be performed by a GE authorized Service Engineer.

  2. Verify the Brain Coil is properly positioned.

  3. Verify there are no loops in the cables.

  4. Verify no metal or ferromagnetic object (such as a safety pin, hair pin, etc.) is close to the coil, patient, or magnet.

  5. Verify the center frequency is within the frequency adjustment range for the system.

  6. Verify the R1, R2 and TG values from the pre-scan are within normally expected ranges.

  7. If not already done, proceed to Brain Coil Setup for MCQA Test.

  8. If the values obtained do not fall within normal operating parameters, remove the Brain Coil from the magnet bore, and perform a scan with a different coil.

    • If the scan completes successfully with the substitute coil, acquire a second scan using another Brain Coil of the same type (receive-only, phased array, or transmit/receive) and the system coil selection. If image quality is visibly improved, the problem may be with the Brain Coil.

    • If still having the same issues, a problem may exist with the PET/MR system.

    • If image quality is unimproved, there may be a system problem related to this type of Brain Coil.

    Contact GE for further assistance.

Topic ID: id_SL13401884-1243983

Artifacts

Problem: A black line or signal void exists on the image.

Possible Solutions:

  1. Confirm no metallic objects are located nearby, outside the FOV (especially important on images utilizing fat saturation.

  2. If fat saturation is used, verify the Center Frequency fine adjustment was optimized.

Topic ID: id_SL13401887-1243983

Cable Continuity Checks

Topic ID: id_SL13401888-1243983

External Cable

Problem: System will not recognize the coil, or reports MC bias errors with the coil attached.

Possible Solution: Perform cable continuity checks as described below.

Topic ID: id_SL13401852-1243983

RF Signal Channels Check

Notice
The center pins of the receive coaxial connectors on the system cable are extremely fragile. Do not attempt to make any measurements at these center pins.

  1. With the coil cable disconnected, use a DVM to ensure an open circuit condition exists between the designated pin and Ground (GND) for each signal channel.

    Table 1. Coil Cable Connector Readings
    Signal Channel Cable Connector System Connection
    MC-1A7GND
    MC-2A6
    MC-3A5
    MC-4A4
    MC-5A3
    MC-6A2
    MC-7Pin 2
    MC-8A1
    GNDNOTE: All outer (A) shells and Pins 1 and 3 are common to GND.

  2. If one or more readings indicate a short, replace the Brain Coil. If all readings indicate an open circuit condition, proceed to the next section.

Topic ID: id_SL13401893-1243983

PIN Diode Control Channels Check

  1. With the coil cable disconnected, use a DVM to determine continuity between the designated pins for each control channel, and verify the control pins are not shorted to GND.

    Table 2. External Cable Readings
    Signal Channel Cable Connector System Connection
    MC-1Pin 17J1
    MC-2Pin 16J2
    MC-3Pin 15J3
    MC-4Pin 14J4
    MC-5Pin 4J5
    MC-6Pin 5J6
    MC-7Pin 6J7
    MC-8Pin 7J8
    GNDNOTE: All outer [A] shells and Pins 1 and 3 are common to GND.K1 to K8, M6

  2. If one or more readings are >3 ohms, replace the Brain Coil. If all readings are <3 ohms, proceed to the next section.

Topic ID: id_SL13401902-1243983

Mechanical Parts Wear

Inspect the Brain Coil and cable for visible signs of wear. If the coil housings do not seat with each other or if the latch mechanism does not work properly, contact GE Service.
