Leak detection sensor theory and troubleshooting

Topic ID: id_SL2190580-1067795

Theory

Leak detection consists of a series of cable segments positioned in different areas of the PGR cabinet and joined by interconnecting cables and connectors. Each cable segment is a two-conductor, ladder-line design whereby the parallel conductors are separated a set distance apart the length of the cable by an insulating dielectric. These cables are located in front of the PDU, above the PDU, and under the RF amplifier. They are interconnected as shown below.

The Auxiliary board or PEM connects to one end of interconnected string of cable segments and constantly measures the total line impedance. Any moisture on the line (from a leak, etc.) results in an impedance that causes the Auxiliary board or PEM to shunt trip the PDU main breaker, which removes power from the input of the PDU.

DANGER
Electric shock hazard
Lethal voltages are present in the PGR Power Distribution Unit (PDU) or gradient cabinet.
Lock out and tag out the power at the main disconnect panel (MDP) before proceeding.

Opening any of the connections between line segments, however, causes the Auxiliary board or PEM to latch into failing state whereby an error is logged and scanning is inhibited. Opening a line segment does not result in PDU main breaker trip. Recovery from latched state is only possible by reconnecting segments and then cycling power to the Auxiliary board or PEM (Gradient 48V Control on front of PDU).

The terminating block contains a special diode so that the line measures as a continuous loop to the Auxiliary board or PEM.

Failure mode 1 – PDU main breaker trip

Refer to Cable Interconnections, and visually inspect for moisture on lines. LOTO incoming power to PGR cabinet and repair any leaks. Use absorbent material to thoroughly dry any moisture from above, below, and around leak detection lines.

Topic ID: id_SL2190718-1067795

Failure mode 2 – PDU main breaker trip, lines dry

DANGER
Shock hazard and death by electrocution
Standing water inside cabinets poses an electrocution risk.
Under no circumstances should you proceed unless you have visually inspected and confirmed that no standing water is present inside the cabinet.
  1. On the PDU, turn off the breaker labeled Gradient 420V Power.
  2. Disconnect each line detection segment in the order shown, and then reset the PDU main breaker. It is normal for a disconnect error to be logged when this is done.
  3. The point at which the breaker does not trip indicates a faulty line segment in the previous section.
  4. Replace or repair the faulty line segment.
  5. If all cables (including connection at location 8) are disconnected and the PDU main breaker is still tripping, then replace the Auxiliary board or PEM.
  6. Confirm reattachment of all circuit connectors.
  7. After replacement, cycle power to the Auxiliary board or PEM (Gradient 48V Control on PDU) to reset it from latched fault condition.

Failure mode 3 – PDU leak sensor disconnect error; scanning inhibited, no main breaker trip

There are several possible causes for this failure mode. Follow the steps in the sections below to determine the cause of the error.

Topic ID: id_SL5599834-1067795

Terminator connection

  1. Sometimes the terminator on the leak strip is bumped and disconnected when the PDU front cover is removed for service. It may appear connected when it is not.
  2. Verify that the terminator is connected by pushing down the tabs on top with a small screwdriver, and simultaneously forcing the wire into its opening.
Topic ID: id_SL5599838-1067795

Cable connections

  1. Check that the cable is attached at J4 on the Auxiliary board or at J6 on the PEM.
  2. Trace the other end of the cable and confirm that it is labeled Leak Sensor 1-A and is attached to end of leak detector segment routed in front of the RF amplifier.
Topic ID: id_SL5599839-1067795

Resistance checks

  1. Complete the resistance checks of leak sensor circuit to find the location of high impedance.
    1. Disconnect the cable connector inspected earlier and labeled Leak Sensor 1-A.
    2. Use a digital multimeter to measure resistance from the terminal block contact to the corresponding connector conductor. You may need to swap measurement points at one end in order to locate the correct line.
    3. The resistance between two points on the same line should measure 10 ohms ± 10 ohms.
    4. Repeat the measurement on the opposite line, and confirm the same result.
  2. If both measurements are good, test the Auxiliary board or PEM.
    1. Reattach the leak detection cable connection in the front of the RF amplifier.
    2. From CSD Utilities, perform a Host PC shutdown. Confirm that the ICNs also shut down.
    3. Place all circuit breakers in the OFF (down) position.
    4. Rotate main PDU circuit breaker (not shown) in the OFF position to remove input power from the PDU.
    5. Roll up the end of a paper towel and wet a 2 inch (5 cm) section with water.
    6. Wrap at least one turn of the moistened section of towel tightly around the exposed middle section of the leak detection line located in front of the PDU.
    7. Rotate the main PDU circuit breaker in ON position to apply input power to PDU.
    8. Place the breaker labeled Gradient 48V Control on PDU in the ON (up) position. This will power on fans and the Auxiliary board or PEM.
    9. Wait 10 seconds for the PDU main breaker to trip.
      1. If the PDU main breaker trips, repair or replace the terminated leak detection segment (section that was tested) located in front of the PDU.
      2. If the PDU main breaker does not trip, retest the Auxiliary board or PEM. If the PDU main breaker still does not trip, replace the Auxiliary board or PEM.
  3. If a bad measurement is seen when doing the leak sensor circuit resistance checks, move to the next connection segment in the leak circuit. Keep repeating the measurement test at each segment, in the order shown below, until you locate the faulty connection. All measurements referenced to termination block.
  4. Replace or repair the faulty connection.
  5. Confirm reattachment of all circuit connectors.
  6. If necessary, thoroughly dry the dampened section of the leak detect line.
  7. Cycle power to the Auxiliary board or PEM (Gradient 48V control on PDU) to recover it from latched fault condition.
  8. Any remaining PDU circuit breakers can now be put back in ON position.
