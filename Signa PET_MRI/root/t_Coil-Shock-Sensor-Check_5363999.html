  • Planned Maintenance
    • Planned Maintenance (PM) schedule overview

      Planned Maintenance (PM) tasks are developed from design requirement and operations/use assumptions to provide for the minimum required maintenance that maintains specified equipment design performance, high image quality, and safety.

    • Planned Maintenance (PM) schedules

    • PM Procedures
      • Deionization procedure
      • Checking InSite connectivity and ProDiags
      • Inspecting the cryogen vent

        Inspect the cryogen Ventglas connection and clamp for damage, and weep hole for debris.

      • Checking InSite Remote Service Platform (RSvP) connectivity and ProDiags functionality

        Make sure the Remote Service Platform (RSvP) has connectivity and ProDiags is functioning properly.

      • Checking the oxygen monitor

        References to various Oxygen Monitor Subsystem Manuals.

      • Doing an MRU yearly service check (MRU, ERU, or EDU PM testing matrix)

        The model number is listed on the side of the Magnet Rundown Unit (MRU), Emergency Rundown Unit (ERU), or Emergency Discharge Unit (EDU). Refer to the appropriate vendor manual available from the online documentation library for applicable testing procedures.

      • Checking the pneumatic patient alert system

        Makes sure the stand-alone pneumatic patient alerts system is operating properly.

      • Checking and filling hydraulic fluid

        Checks and adjusts the hydraulic fluid level for the dockable patient table.

      • Cleaning or replacing the body coil blower air flow filter

        Clean and/or replace the body coil blower air flow filter.

      • Checking the patient handling system with the laser leveling tool

        The proper alignment of the entire patient handling system is important for good image quality output from the GE MR scanners.

      • MDP power and ground and battery replacement label check

        MDP power and ground connections must be tight in order to avoid system failures.

      • MDP Power and Ground Connection Checks
      • Checking and adjusting the patient intercom functionality

        GOC Audio Assembly (GOCAA) controls are adjusted at factory for best sound quality. This document describes how to check and confirm volume and quality of sound heard from speaker in the magnet bore and from speaker at the Scan Control and Intercom Module (SCIM). Directions for sound adjustments are also provided if needed.

      • Verifying the patient table movement

        Make sure the patient table controls operate correctly.

      • Adjusting the cradle/table release block

        Adjust the cradle release block position and the cradle release block retraction.

      • Doing the Leak Sensor functional check

        Test the functionality of the leak sensors.

      • Doing the Universal Power Monitor (UPM) functional check (Head and Body)

        Do the UPM head and body functional check to make sure the measured RF power is within the expected range.

      • Dual drive quadrature tool

        The dual drive (DD) quadrature tool is used to compensate for the amplitude and phase differences in the transmit channels.

      • Doing DQA calibration with the DQA III phantom

        The DQA tool determines correct gradient polarity and correct gradient wiring by scanning a series of axial and coronal images of the DQA III phantom for systems below sentence MR 31.0 or sites that do not have a 17cm Sphere with Spout (SWS) phantom.

      • Doing DQA calibration with the Sphere With Spout (SWS) phantom

        Details the steps needed to run the Gradient Calibration Tool (GCT) for systems running MR31.0 and higher and that possess the 17cm Sphere with Spout (SWS) phantom. GCT does the same checks and produces the same output as DQA.

      • Testing PAC leakage current

        Measure and record the PAC leakage current data.

      • Checking the gradient and system PDU power and ground connection

        PDU power and ground connections need to be tight in order to avoid system failures.

      • PDU power and ground connection checks
      • Setting the time, time zone, and date

        Set the time, time zone, and date in Guided Install.

      • Replacing and cleaning the blower box filter

        Clean and replace the blower box filter.

      • Heat Exchanger Cabinet (HEC) installation
      • Checking the ECG leads

        Test the electrical connections on the ECG cables.

      • T-file cleanup

        Use the T File Cleanup tool to delete old T test data files.

      • Inspecting the cryocooler adsorber

        Inspect the cryocooler adsorber on-site.

      • Checking the RF screen room door

        Make sure the RF screen room door is functioning correctly.

      • Checking the emergency exhaust vent

        Check to make sure that the exhaust fan is running.

      • Cleaning or replacing the P-port receptacles (ODU connectors)

        The P-port receptacle must be cleaned and lubricated on each of the system P-ports.

      • Doing a compressor pressure check

        Do a check of the cryocooler compressor supply pressure.

      • GE OR-compatible MR table option
      • CPC Multi-Nuclear Spectroscopy (MNS) cabinet filter maintenance
      • Inspecting and topping off PET chiller coolant

        Inspect the amount of PET chiller coolant and, if needed, add more coolant.

      • Cradle Release Block Adjustment
      • PET detector calibration

        This procedure calibrates the entire PET detector module ring, or integrates a single detector module.

      • PET Daily Quality Assurance (DQA) check

        Select the software version for which you want to do a PET Daily Quality Assurance (DQA) check.

      • MR–PET Image Alignment VQC Calibration
      • PET Normalization Correction Calibration
      • PET 3D Well Counter Correction (WCC)

        Steps on how to do a PET 3D Well Counter Correction (WCC), which is a cross-calibration to the site’s dose calibrator.

      • PET Non-Patient Objects Fiducial Calibration

        The PET non-patient objects fiducial (NPOF) calibration checks the PET imaging.

      • Oxygen Monitor System Calibration
      • Coil Shock Sensor Check
      • ECG leads installation and functional check
      • Extracting coolant from the PET chiller and manifold

        Extract coolant from the PET chiller and manifold.

      • Installing the bore leak sensor
      • PAC Assembly Leakage Current Test
      • Examining the PET workstation battery replacement

        Look at the battery replacement label on the PET workstation to see if the battery should be replaced.

Procedure

  1. Notice
    Only coils manufactured and/or distributed by General Electric should have a shock sensor sticker, and each coil should have only one shock sensor installed. (Look at the rating plate on the coil for this information.) Coils not purchased through General Electric (E-Cats) are not included, and should not have a shock sensor installed.
    Check if the site has any coils listed in either table below.
    1. Look for physical damage to the coil that could affect its functional effectiveness. Refer to the coil operator manual for more details if necessary. If there is physical damage, such as a cracked or broken piece off the coil, proceed directly to Step 6.
    2. Check the coil for a shock sensor sticker:

    • If the coil does not have a shock sensor sticker, follow the Coil Shock Sensor Installation procedure to install a shock sensor on the coil.

    • If the shock sensor sticker is present on the coil, continue to Step 2.

  2. Check if the shock sensor was triggered (center tube turns red as shown in illustration on the right side).

    • If the shock sensor was triggered (center tube is red) or contaminated in any way, continue to Step 3.

    • If the shock sensor was not triggered (center tube is white as shown in illustration on the left side above), continue to 1.

    Note

    Any red or pink color in the center tube indicates a triggered shock sensor as shown below; tube may not be completely red.

  3. Remove the triggered shock sensor from the coil and dispose of it according to local procedures.
  4. Verbally educate the customer on safe use of coils and implications on the warranty for mishandling of coils.
  5. Re-qualify the coil by running the Multi-Coil Quality Assurance Tool test.

    • If the coil passes the MCQA test, apply a new shock sensor sticker following Coil Shock Sensor Installation, and continue to 1.

    • If the coil fails the MCQA test, continue to Step 6.

  6. Notice
    Remove the shock sensor before returning the damaged coil.
    Replace the coil according to replacement procedures. Fill in the Coil Data Collection Sheet, indicating what failed on the coil, and include a copy inside the shipping container with the damaged coil.
    1. Charge the customer for repair/replacement of the damaged coil per local procedures.
    2. Verbally educate the customer on safe use of coils and implications on the warranty for mishandling of coils.

Finalization

  1. Make sure the above procedure is completed for all coils at the customer site listed in 1.5T Coils with Shock Sensor or 3.0T Coils with Shock Sensor. Return the coils for use to the customer.
  2. Reuse the original packaging of the coil shock sensors for transportation.
  3. If running low, order a new box of shock sensor stickers through SSP Purchasing two to three weeks ahead of the next PM to allow time for delivery.
