Deionizing the gradient coil coolant

Follow these steps to deionize and filter the gradient coil coolant in the Integrated Cooling Cabinet (ICC).

Procedure

  1. Open the Integrated Cooling Cabinet (ICC) door.
  2. Open the deionization kit case and put it on the floor in front of the cabinet. Position the case so the pump is closest to the floor and horizontal to the floor.
    Note Make sure that the filter tool is on the floor below the ICC tanks.
  3. Uncoil the hoses by unfastening the Velcro straps.
  4. Using the wrench tool included with the kit, unscrew both the deionization and filter housings. Each housing may need to be pulled away from the case to fit the wrench behind it.
  5. Open the package for the deionization cartridge and put it into the housing.
    1. Make sure that the arrow on the deionization cartridge is pointing up.
    2. Screw the deionization filter housing back into the top labeled Deionization.
      Make sure to fully tighten it.
  6. Open the package for the filter cartridge and put it into the housing.
    1. Screw the filter housing back into the top labeled Filter.
      Make sure to fully tighten it.
  7. Remove the cap from the Gradient Cooling Unit (GCU) tank fill port. Connect the tank funnel extension to the fill port and thread the deionization tank adapter into it. Open the vent on the tank adapter.
  8. If it is not already off, remove the yellow cap from the GCU drain that is labeled drain valve dirt trap. Then connect the deionization kit suction line to the dirt separator drain port.
  9. Connect the deionization kit return line quick disconnect to the deionization tank adapter.
  10. Connect the power cords.
    1. Connect the power cord to the extension power cord.
    2. Connect the extension cord to the deionization kit power outlet located on the right side of the ICC control unit and turn on the service outlet breaker.
  11. Monitor the suction line.
    Coolant should be trickling into the suction line.
    • If coolant is in the suction line at the pump, the pump is sufficiently primed.
    • If no coolant is in the suction line, make sure that the return line is connected properly to the GCU tank fill port. See note below.
    DO NOT attempt to run the deionization kit pump until the pump is primed.
    Note Briefly start the pump for 3 to 5 seconds until it has caught prime. The pump must be primed to prevent damage. Make sure there is flow by observing the coolant filling the deionization and filter housings. If flow stops, stop the pump to prevent damage. Disconnect the line from the reservoir. Remove the filter housing and drain.
    • Make sure the filter tool is on the floor below ICC tanks
    • Make sure hoses are not kinked
    • Make sure the vent on the return hose is open
    • Repeat this step until the pump is primed
  12. (For software version 30 and later) To monitor the resistivity progress of the gradient coolant, do the following:
    1. In the Common Service Desktop (CSD), click the Utilities tab.
    2. Select Machine Data Viewers from the left column, and then Click here to start this tool.
    3. In the Machine Data Viewers window, select Cooling Cabinet Data from the Files_in menu, and click Accept.
    4. Select the Cooling Cabinet radio button in the Data Viewer Selection column, and click View.
    5. In the Cooling Cabinet Viewer window, select Grad Coil Coolant Resistivity.
    6. Scroll to the bottom of the Gradient Coil Coolant Resistivity Data view and read the resistivity data. Click Refresh every 5 to 10 seconds to read the latest data.
      Note Each time the Refresh button is clicked, the scroll bar needs to be moved to the bottom to see the latest data.
    • The process is complete when the resistivity reaches 600 kΩ-cm. Turn off the service outlet breaker.
    • If the gradient coolant resistance is not within the specification, but does not change much, do a check that the GCU is correctly working by doing a check of the pressure gauge.
  13. Disconnect the deionization kit hoses.
    Note Coolant may spill out from the hoses when disconnecting. Put a service tank within reach before disconnecting.
    1. Disconnect the deionization kit return line from the tank adapter at the tank fill port. Then drain the hose into the service tank.
    2. Disconnect the deionization kit suction line from the GCU dirt separator drain port. Then drain the hose into the service tank.
  14. Put the yellow cap back on the GCU dirt separator drain port.
  15. Remove the deionization tank adapter from the GCU tank fill port.
    CAUTION
    Hazardous chemicals
    Chemicals may cause injury.
    Wear goggles and gloves when pouring chemicals.
  16. Put the funnel into the GCU tank fill port and restore coolant from the service tank to the GCU tank.
  17. Locate the gradient coil deionization chemical additive and gradually pour into the GCU tank until the resistivity is within the range of 10 kΩ-cm to 15 kΩ-cm and is stable for at least 15 minutes. To monitor the resistivity progress, see t_DeionizingTheGradientCoilCoolant.html#id_2001926__step-resistivity or Step 12.
    Note If the chemical contents are filled into the tank too fast, the resistivity value may drop rapidly and reach a trip level that will interrupt a customer scan. The resistivity should be in the 10 kΩ-cm to 15 kΩ-cm range after adding the chemical additive.
    Note Dispose of the deionization chemicals per local regulation.
  18. Remove the funnel and replace the cap on the GCU tank fill port.
